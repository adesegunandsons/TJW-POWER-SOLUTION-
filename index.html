Solar Buying Guides

Plain-language answers before you buy.

Guides

General guidance to help you ask better questions. Every home and business is different, so contact us for a recommendation based on your own power needs.

How to estimate your home power consumption

Start with a list of every appliance you want to run.

  • Find each appliance's power in watts (W) on its label.
  • Estimate how many hours a day you use it.
  • Multiply: watts × hours = watt-hours (Wh) per day. Add them up. Divide by 1,000 for kilowatt-hours (kWh).

Illustrative example: a 10 W bulb used for 5 hours uses 50 Wh. A 100 W TV used for 5 hours uses 500 Wh.

Your prepaid meter token history also shows how many kWh you buy, and your generator fuel use shows what you currently spend. Appliances with motors, such as air conditioners, freezers and pumps, usually use the most.

How to choose the right inverter size
  • Add up the watts of everything that may run at the same time. That is your peak load.
  • Allow for start-up surges. Motors in fridges, freezers, pumps and air conditioners can draw several times their running power for a moment. Ask your supplier about this.
  • Leave some headroom, for example 20 to 30 percent, for appliances you may add later.
  • Check the real watt (kW) rating, not just the kVA figure. They are not the same.
  • Make sure the inverter suits your battery type and voltage, and whether you need single-phase or three-phase.
Differences between battery types

Generally speaking:

  • Flooded (tubular) lead-acid: usually the lowest upfront cost, but needs regular maintenance such as topping up with distilled water, and has less usable capacity.
  • Sealed lead-acid (AGM or gel): little maintenance, but typically a shorter life than lithium.
  • Lithium (for example LiFePO4): usually costs more upfront, is lighter, and offers more usable capacity and a longer cycle life, with a built-in battery management system.

Always compare the manufacturer's datasheet: usable capacity, cycle life and warranty. And confirm the battery is compatible with your inverter.

What to know before buying solar panels
  • Check roof space, direction, shade and whether the roof can safely hold the panels.
  • Compare panel wattage, datasheets and warranty (product warranty and performance warranty).
  • Ask what mounting, cables, DC protection, surge protection and earthing are included.
  • Think about dust, cleaning, and security against theft.
  • Plan for future expansion before you buy.
  • Get an itemised written quotation.
How to choose a solar system for a shop or office
  • List what must stay on: lights, computers, POS, network, fridges or freezers, and air conditioning.
  • Note when you use power. Daytime-heavy businesses can benefit well from solar.
  • Decide how many hours of backup you want at night.
  • Check the available roof or ground space.
  • Consider a phased plan if the full system is beyond your budget now.
Questions to ask before paying for installation
  • What exactly is included? Ask for an itemised list with brands and models.
  • What warranty applies to each item, and who handles claims?
  • Can I see previous work, or speak to previous customers?
  • What is the payment schedule? Be careful about paying everything upfront.
  • Who will do the installation, and how are safety and testing handled?
  • What is not included (cables, civil works, delivery)?
  • What is the timeline, and what after-sales support is available?
  • Is everything in writing?
